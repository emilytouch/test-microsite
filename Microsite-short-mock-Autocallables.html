FAQs

When will the funds launch?

Only after their registration statements become effective. Sign up to be notified when ACSP, ACQQ and ACRT begin trading.

Where can I buy these funds?

ACSP, ACQQ and ACRT trade on Nasdaq and can be bought through most standard brokerage accounts that offer ETFs.

What is an autocallable?

An autocallable is a note linked to an index that can pay income on set observation dates. If conditions are met on an observation date, the note may be "called," or repaid early. Otherwise it runs to maturity.

Where does the income come from?

From premiums paid for taking on defined downside risk, not from dividends or interest. The funds intend to distribute monthly, but distributions are not guaranteed, may vary and may include return of capital.

What does "laddered" mean?

Instead of one note, each fund tracks a ladder of autocallables with staggered dates. As notes are called or mature, the strategy reinvests in new ones, which helps address concentration and timing risks.

What is the 35% principal barrier?

At maturity, a note's principal is protected only if its index has not fallen more than 35%. If the index closes below the barrier at maturity, principal is fully exposed. For example, a 45% decline means a 45% loss on that note. The barrier does not stop the ETF's market price from moving day to day.

What's the difference between the three funds?

All three use the same laddered autocallable strategy. The difference is the linked index: ACSP tracks the S&P 500, ACQQ the Nasdaq-100 and ACRT the Russell 2000. Use Compare all above to see them side by side.

Why an ETF instead of individual notes?

Autocallables have traditionally meant researching, buying and managing individual notes. These ETFs package a full ladder into a single ticker that trades like any other ETF.
